# A. Documentary thesis

**Working title:** *The Insane Real Engineering of the Nazi Enigma Machine*

**Central question (the film's one open loop that everything serves):**
> How can pressing the same key produce a different letter every time — and how could anyone ever break a machine like that?

**Thesis.** Enigma was not magic and not a computer. It was a handful of simple parts — a keyboard, wires, three turning wheels, a mirror-like reflector and a board of cables — arranged so that *the machine changes itself after every letter*. That single idea turned a schoolboy cipher into ~159 quintillion possible setups. It was broken by the same kind of thinking in reverse: Polish mathematicians treated the wiring as algebra, and Turing and Welchman built a machine that did not search for the right answer but **eliminated impossible ones**, using the one thing Enigma could never do — turn a letter into itself.

**What the viewer can do at the end.** Mentally rebuild the machine: trace a current from key to lamp; explain why the rotors step (and double-step); say why the same setting decrypts; compute the keyspace; explain a crib, a menu loop and why the Bombe's "stop" still needed a human.

**Tone.** Engineering documentary, not war drama. Warm brass-and-bakelite macro photography for the machine; clean, quiet diagrams for the maths; restrained history with every claim sourced.

**Priority order** (from the brief): historical accuracy → technical accuracy → first-principles teaching → visual clarity → cinematic quality → entertainment.
